# Rules-story editorial personality

This is the hard-coded default for fictional storytelling, uncanny survival instructions and Bradikinin-like requests. Do not turn it into a science lesson. Use the creator reference https://www.youtube.com/@BradikininVideos as the style target, with original scripts and assets.

Structure: an impossible event involving an everyday thing; an immediate instruction; seven numbered rules; escalating consequences; a reversal that changes the meaning of an earlier rule; a disturbing closing consequence. Each rule needs a concrete action or prohibition, a reason to care and a consequence. Keep geography, objects and rule logic consistent. Avoid repetitive paraphrases and padding. The narrator speaks directly to the viewer with calm procedural confidence and occasional dry, restrained humour. Do not use an excited marketing voice or force laughter into Ming delivery.

Fiction stays identifiable as fiction. Research any factual statements and distinguish them from invented lore. Do not copy a reference episode's plot, wording, institutional identity or presenter voice. A reference URL supplies style direction, not permission to download its footage. A researcher can provide factual background; the writer invents the scenario and rules and marks that material as original fiction in research.md.

Keep SCRIPT.md clean and use the normal script/visual-plan file structure. Events, scene IDs, spoken boundaries, prototype and assets still need to be explicit in VISUALS.md. For this genre a scene's mechanism means the visible rule, violation or consequence; it need not be a scientific demonstration. Titles pair a concrete anomaly with a short command. Thumbnails use the central anomaly and a readable warning, matching DESIGN.md; avoid the explainer thumbnail study's visual style.

## Titles and thumbnails

Use the channel's recurring packaging grammar as a flexible pattern, not a fill-in-the-blanks template: `If Your [familiar thing] Ever [specific impossible event]… [short uppercase instruction]`. The title names one concrete anomaly and gives a direct action, so viewers understand the premise immediately while still wondering what happens next. A concise statement of the same structure is acceptable when it reads more naturally. Keep the instruction accurate to the approved story and make it a real command from the story's rules; do not invent a new prohibition, negate an affirmative rule, or reveal the reversal. Do not reuse a reference video's distinctive wording or premise.

The thumbnail should make the anomaly legible at a glance: one large, original, recognisable subject or device, a simple supporting character or clue when useful, and a short heavy uppercase command. Prefer the profile's near-black stage, white subject/type and one restrained red emphasis; use other accent colours only when they carry story meaning. Keep title and thumbnail complementary: the title can state the impossible event while the thumbnail foregrounds the action or unanswered danger. Do not rely on tiny details, a miniature scene, or a second paragraph of copy. Check the exported image at the matching feed size (180×320 for vertical, 320×180 for landscape, 320×320 for square). Use original art and type; reference thumbnails are study material, never production assets.

Write a specific, accurate description that adds context without promising an outcome the film does not deliver. Record title, thumbnail headline, description and click motivation in the thumbnail brief, and have an independent reviewer assess both the individual options and how the winning title/image work together.

Every numbered rule needs its exact on-screen command and synchronised spoken captions, tied to the action shown. Write that display text and cue explicitly in VISUALS.md. Use the frame-study design specification; the reference is more textured and compositionally varied than a pure black/white/red icon deck. For Shorts, follow the actual portrait framing and caption placement. Do not claim sound or font measurements that the visual study did not establish.

Length adaptation: the seven numbered rules framework belongs to the long-form episode grammar. An actual reference Short concentrates on one incident and a few instructions. For future short-form briefs, choose a coherent short incident rather than automatically cramming a full seven-rule episode into the runtime. If the brief or approved script explicitly includes all seven, preserve that choice and pace it clearly; any script change needs the normal review/approval. Mix relatable factual details with clearly fictional framing only when the factual assertions are sourced. The frame study does not verify every factual claim in the reference videos.
