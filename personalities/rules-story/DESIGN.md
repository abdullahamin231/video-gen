# Rules-story design: fixed Bradikinin-style visual grammar

Use a near-black full-frame stage, stark white simplified pictograms, and red reserved for danger, negation and violations. Default palette: background #050505, foreground #F5F5F0, warning #E1192D, subdued archive labels #777770. Use a bold neutral sans for commands and numbers and small monospaced archive metadata. These tokens are our initial reproducible specification, not measured samples of the creator's exact font files or colours.

Draw simple original human, animal and household-object silhouettes with consistent stroke/shape proportions. Keep faces and detail minimal. One or two large figures or objects dominate a shot. Preserve large areas of black around them. Do not import photographs, commercial footage, complex illustrations, gradients, colourful explainer cards, hanging price tags or rubber-stamp verdicts.

Build the reference's graphic structure: a restrained episode/file label at top left, subtle timecode at top right, a seven-step rule rail near the top, and a bold instruction or warning with pictograms below. Use original labels and identifiers. Keep metadata quieter than the central action. Captions may sit at the bottom in a consistent reserved rail; never overlap a figure or essential instruction. Do not reproduce the reference channel's logo or watermark.

Use deliberate cuts, minimal position changes, selective red strike-throughs and occasional short reveals. A figure can hold while the narration develops tension. Do not force every spoken word into a bouncing entrance or scientific cause-and-effect diagram. Let a sudden change of silhouette, orientation, scale or warning colour carry the violation. Keep effects sparse and seek-safe; any noise or glitch must be deterministic. Consider silence and sparse original/cleared sound instead of a continuous upbeat music bed.

The seven-rule rail updates on the corresponding spoken rule. A meaningful visual state should accompany each new instruction or consequence. A twist must visibly reinterpret an established subject. Scene handoffs can be hard cuts. The final frame should leave the central anomaly unresolved or newly threatening.

Before a complete episode, use the normal prototype checkpoint to lock the style: an instruction state, its violation/consequence and the next rule transition. Reference fidelity is judged against this fixed grammar. Typography, timing and sound remain subject to the person's prototype feedback; never claim an exact measured match without a proper reference study.
