Create original uncanny fiction around a familiar situation. Use rules, violations and consequences to
build tension, choosing their number and order to serve the story. Keep geography, recurring subjects
and causal logic intelligible. Distinguish invented lore from factual research internally; never present
invented survival advice as verified fact.

Tell the story through staged action. Show subjects acting, interacting and reacting as events unfold.
Use animation to communicate intention, cause, consequence and emotion. Camera movement, transitions
and typography should support the action rather than substitute for it. Choose pacing, imagery, sound
and deliberate stillness to sustain unease.

Maintain a coherent visual world and consistent craft from beginning to end. Preserve the identity,
proportions and level of detail of recurring subjects and environments. Make spatial relationships and
physical interactions readable, including in stylised treatments. References may guide tone and visual
language without dictating the plot, a shot template or copied assets.

Inspect the complete sequence for narrative clarity, visual continuity, composition and purposeful
motion. Review playback to judge animation and pacing; individual frames and successful technical
checks alone cannot establish film quality.

Create new projects with `--profile rules-story`. The shared transport contract is `docs/orchestrator.md`.

Titles follow one shape: a concrete trigger in sentence case ("If your [object, device, app, person, place or
event] ever [specific anomaly]…"), an ellipsis, then a short instruction in capitals that tells the viewer to
do the opposite of what seems sensible. The trigger can be anything a viewer could plausibly meet in ordinary
life, online or in the story's world; it need not be domestic. Make the anomaly specific enough to picture,
and let the instruction imply the rule and its consequence without naming them.

This shape governs the title only. It may suggest a premise, but it does not constrain the story: you keep
full creative authority over the premise, structure, imagery, tone and ending, and should invent the most
effective original scary story you can. Examples: "If Your Kettle Ever Boils At 4:00… DON'T POUR IT"; "If
Your Phone Ever Types A Message You Didn't Write… DON'T SEND IT"; "If The Bus Driver Ever Skips Your Stop
And Says Your Name… STAY ON BOARD". Do not reuse a premise, object or instruction from an existing video.

Use [Motion Explainer](../../docs/motion-explainer.md) as an optional camera and motion reference
for staged action, reveals and transitions. Adapt pacing to tension and preserve visual continuity.
