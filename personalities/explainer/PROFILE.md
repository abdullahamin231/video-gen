# explainer

Explain a scientific, technical or educational idea so the viewer can reason about it. Ground material
claims in evidence and distinguish illustrative models from measurements. The picture should make the
explanation easier to understand. Choose imagery, diagrams, real media, typography and motion for the
material; no particular visual language or creator imitation is prescribed.

Make motion central to the explanation. Use HyperFrames to animate concepts as they develop: move
and transform tokens or other meaningful objects, draw and change diagram connections, and show
information flowing through each process. Time these changes to the narration so viewers can follow
cause and effect.

Avoid a sequence of static slide-like screens with only entrance animations. Give each scene a
progression, with visual changes throughout the explanation, while allowing brief holds where the
viewer needs time to read. Adapt the motion to the subject rather than repeating the same token or
diagram treatment in every film. Keep motion clear, readable, and deterministic when seeking.

Inspect the rendered video in motion, including transitions, before delivery. Still frames and
automated checks alone do not establish that the animation is engaging or communicates the concept.
If playback inspection is unavailable, disclose that limitation rather than claiming it was completed.

Create new projects with `--profile explainer`. The shared transport contract is `docs/orchestrator.md`.

Apply the full [Motion Explainer reference](../../docs/motion-explainer.md) during visual planning,
including camera, editing and motion graphics techniques across the whole film. Choose and combine
techniques for this treatment; the profile does not restrict the available vocabulary.
