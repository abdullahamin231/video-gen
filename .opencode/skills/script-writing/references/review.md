# Fresh script review

Review the brief, SCRIPT.md, VISUALS.md, research.md, SOURCES.md and saved sources in a fresh context. Do not use the writer's self-assessment
or lint scores as evidence of quality. Write your own `review.md` in the script's output directory. Do not rewrite
the script yourself; give the writer concrete defects and desired outcomes.

Read it first as the intended viewer, then check the evidence. Address these questions with exact quoted lines:

- Does the opening pull a viewer in? For a long explainer, check the cold open in `craft.md`: a specific scene rather
  than the topic, a narrator who reacts like a person, the belief people hold and why it is wrong or incomplete (real
  and paid off, not staged), and a plain promise within about the first minute. An opening that starts on a definition,
  a history or the topic itself is a `revise` finding for a long explainer. Does the ending fulfill the promise? Where
  would attention drop, and why?
- Can the viewer follow the explanation, reproduce the tutorial's result, or understand the story's events? Name any
  missing step, repetition or abrupt transition. Judge the requested genre, not a preference for a particular voice.
- Does every material factual claim follow from the saved evidence? Check causal claims, comparisons, sample scope,
  denominators and caveats. Trace central measurements to the primary source. If only a secondary account was read,
  require that attribution and uncertainty in the script. Distinguish evidence from opinion and anecdote.
- Are first-person experiences supported by creator notes? A source's experience must not become the narrator's.
  Opinions and reactions in the first person are fine; "I did X" without notes is an invented experience (blocking).
- Does it sound natural at the chosen speaking rate, and fit the target budget? Do not reward matching tell counters.
- When the writer chose the length (the brief named none), is it the right one? Name beats a viewer would not miss
  (padding) or steps rushed past (too short), and suggest a target. A badly chosen length is a `revise` finding; a
  requested length is the user's call and is not second-guessed.
- Is `VISUALS.md` buildable by someone who will invent little (see `visuals.md`)? Check that every scene's start and
  event phrases occur in SCRIPT.md exactly, scenes cover the script in order, each central idea has a concrete visible
  cause → consequence → payoff rather than a labelled box or on-screen sentence, real things are named with how to
  find or draw them, and every shown number or claim is supported. Vague direction ("an engaging visual of X") on a
  central idea is blocking; it is what the builders will otherwise produce generically.

Check the three matched packaging alternatives and the selected title, thumbnail headline and short description
in `VISUALS.md` against `docs/video-click-psychology.md`. Identify the audience, the reason to click, how the image
adds to the title, and the exact payoff in the film. Check claims in all three pieces of copy, preserve meaningful
qualifications, and flag missing copy for revision. Benefits and questions are valid; negativity is optional.
An editorial ranking predicts appeal and must not claim measured performance.

Check claims against the saved sources (`SOURCES.md` and `sources/`); do not fetch new material yourself. If a
central claim needs evidence that was never saved, mark the review `incomplete` and name the missing source, so the
host can send the researcher for it.

Record the inspected file paths and any unavailable evidence. For each finding, quote the script line, describe its
effect on the viewer or the unsupported inference, cite the supporting passage where applicable, and suggest a repair.
Mark unsupported central claims, invented experiences, missing explanatory steps and an unfulfilled promise as
blocking; preferences and optional tightening are advisory. Do not turn one unusual phrase into an automatic rejection.

Finish with `pass`, `revise` or `incomplete`, with reasons. A pass requires actually checking the full script and its
material claims, not just the opening or selected numbers. Missing central evidence is incomplete. On re-review,
inspect the changed script and retain prior findings with resolved/unresolved status. Be explicit if you read the text
without hearing an audio performance; mental read-through is not listening.
